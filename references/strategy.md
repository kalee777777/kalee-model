# Strategy analysis — trial playbook

Use this mode for business direction, project selection and resource allocation. Ground the analysis in the user's current principles, relevant decision history and available evidence. This playbook does not add ratified principles or establish predictive accuracy.

## Establish the decision

Read the relevant project records, existing commitments and comparable decisions before asking for information already available. Identify the intended beneficiary, problem, desired business result, time window and actual resource constraints. Separate the goal from a proposed solution: obtaining a paid pilot does not necessarily require building a platform first.

Label material claims as **evidence**, **inference**, or **unknown**, with their source and date. Historical cases establish what happened then; refresh time-sensitive market, price, policy and competitor facts before using them for a current recommendation. Missing budget, capacity, customer demand or unit costs stay unknown.

When citing a precedent, explain its similarities, differences and whether its outcome was reviewed. If the log has no relevant cases, disclose the coverage gap and offer a provisional framework without pretending it represents the user's proven judgment.

## Produce a decision brief

Scale the detail to the decision; small reversible choices do not need a long report. For significant choices, cover:

1. **Objective and constraints:** desired outcome, existing assets, available time and money, competing commitments, irreversible costs.
2. **Credible alternatives:** compare demand evidence, access to customers, delivery economics, validation speed, resource use, opportunity cost and reusable assets. Include maintaining the current approach or deferring when credible. Do not invent weights to create a precise-looking score.
3. **Recommendation and strongest objection:** state the tradeoff, the best argument against the recommendation and when an alternative would be preferable. Do not merely justify the user's favored idea.
4. **Critical assumptions and reversal conditions:** what has to be true, what evidence would test it and what would change the recommendation.
5. **Smallest useful experiment:** audience, action, deliverable, observation window, cost ceiling, success/failure signals and evidence collection. Proposed thresholds must be labeled as proposals; unknown limits do not authorize spending.

End with what is supported, what remains uncertain and the next action within the user's authorization. When a direction is selected and execution authorized, continue with [project planning](project-planning.md) without asking for the same permission again.

Use the user's available domain-research playbooks for specialized evidence. Do not assume this repository includes market-research tools, customer systems or a specific task service.

## Review before handing off

Could another reasonable option win? Is the strongest objection addressed? Are opportunity costs visible? Could the hypothesis be disproved? Are unknown quantities presented honestly? Does the proposed experiment fit the resources actually available?
